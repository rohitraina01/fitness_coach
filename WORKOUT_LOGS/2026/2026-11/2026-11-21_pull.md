### 2. Trainee Biomechanical Diagnostic: "The row really burned my side delts..."
- **Why It Happened**:
  1. *Elbow Flare & Abduction Torque*: On both the heavy 65.0 kg Seated Cable Row and DB Row, fatigue encourages pulling with elbows flared out (60°–90° away from the torso) rather than tucked at 30°–45°. Flaring arms wide forces the lateral deltoid to contract isometrically at high tension to stabilize the humerus against gravity and cable load.
  2. *Scapular Shrugging (Upper Trap Compensation)*: When moving maximum weight (65 kg), the nervous system tries to recruit the upper traps by elevating the shoulder girdle (shrugging toward ears). This transfers tension away from lats directly into upper traps and the lateral/posterior delt tie-in.
  3. *Anatomical Confusion (Rear Delt vs Side Delt)*: The posterior deltoid inserts into the deltoid tuberosity of the humerus, immediately adjacent to the lateral delt. An intense pump and burn in the rear delt (which worked overtime on 65kg rows and DB rows) frequently radiates and feels identical to a side delt burn.
- **The Technical Fix for Future Rowing**:
  - *Tuck Elbows*: Keep elbows angled 30°–45° from the ribcage.
  - *Drive to the Hip Pocket*: Pull the handle/dumbbell toward your lower abdomen/waistband, NEVER up toward your chest or armpit.
  - *Lead with the Elbow Tips*: Treat hands as passive hooks; pull entirely by driving the points of your elbows straight back.
